## What I wrote from the charts (30-minute candles, the 20 hours before and the day), BEFORE counting
1. A flat, narrow box for hours before the biggest hour.
2. The biggest hour starts on a session clock: 07:00-08:00 or 13:00-14:00 London.
3. Big days come in groups (01-22..27, 03-18..23, 06-02..08).
4. The biggest hour mostly carries on the way the hours before it were leaning.

## What I learned
- My eye was wrong on the main point. The "box" is the chart's scale: a 250-pip day squeezes a 50-pip night flat. In pips the hours
  before a big day are busier than usual, not quieter.
- The clock times are where EVERY day's biggest hour sits. No information.
- What is left: big days follow big days (65% against 38%). That says something about SIZE tomorrow and nothing about DIRECTION, and my
  VL test already found A2 no better on such days. I have not checked whether 13 of 20 against 41 of 107 could be chance; it is one of
  seven features looked at, so I treat it as a lean.
- Nothing here is a setup yet. One possible test, NOT run: after a day with a range above its 10-day median, does a both-ways breakout
  of the night range pay after costs? It needs a rule for the side-less entry, 100+ cases counted first, kill lines first.
