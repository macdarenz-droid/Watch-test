## What they share (written from the charts, then counted)
1. THE NIGHT WENT AGAINST THE TRADE, AND KEPT GOING. On most of them price had been moving steadily against my side for hours before 08:00 - not a spike, a one-way
   drift (03-02, 03-19, 04-02, 04-08, 04-16, 04-20, 04-22 over the whole night; 03-09, 03-12, 04-21 in the last two hours) - and in the full stops London simply carried on.
   The pre-entry hour went against me on 11 of the 15 (median -12 pips).
2. Not one of the eight full stops was "in doubt": best price before noon +2 to +18 pips on six of them. The scratches, by contrast, all had +10 to +55 at some point.
3. The afternoon is no consolation and no pattern: at 17:00 price was on my side of the entry on 7 of 15; from noon to 17:00 it moved my way on 9 of 15.
4. The two "with me" pre-hours (03-26 +30, 03-27 +65) are the two where I chased a breakout candle and got the exact extreme - the H (no-chase) shape, which failed its test.
Lesson 1 is the only one that is (a) visible before the entry, (b) present in most of the 15 and (c) about the full stops, which are what hurts.

## Before I believe it: why this review is a biased way to find a rule
- I looked at LOSERS ONLY. Anything common on my mornings will be "shared" by them. Base rate, counted on all 78 signal days without looking at results:
  the night (00:00 -> 08:00 London) ended against my side on 37 days (47%); it did so in a one-way manner (net move at least half of the night's whole range) on 20 days (26%):
  3 in January, 5 in February, 12 in March-to-now. So "the night was against me" describes nearly half of ALL my mornings.
- I have tested the nearest thing before: idea NM (03-31), "skip when the night moved against my side" - on JANUARY ONLY, and it was backwards (the skipped trades were winners).
  The night study on 04-22 added: nights normally go against the previous day's move. So a night against me is the ordinary case, and in January it was the good case.
- All 15 reviewed losers lie between 02-27 and 04-22. A rule drawn from them will fit that stretch by construction. The only part of my history this review did not
  touch is 01-06 .. 02-26 (38 trades) - and for January I already know NM's answer. I expect the lesson to fail there. That is what the test is for.

## Hypothesis
When the night has moved one way against my side by the time I enter (00:00 -> 08:00 London: net move against me, at least half of the night's range), London tends to
continue it, and the A2 trade has clearly lower expectancy - in the months I did not review as well as in the ones I did.

## KILL TEST (explicit). The build can only kill; a survivor gets a forward paper tag (no adoption question before 200 forward trades).
A variant survives only if ALL:
  K1. avg R per A2 signal day at least A2's + 0.05R (all history);
  K2. the days it changes are worse under A2 than the days it leaves alone by at least 0.3R (all history);
  K3. THE DECISIVE LINE: the same gap is at least +0.3R on the part I did NOT review, 01-06 .. 02-26. (The reviewed part was chosen by its losers; a gap there proves nothing.)
  K4. it changes at least 10 days and leaves at least 60% of the days alone. ON1 changes 47% of the days: it FAILS K4 before it is run and cannot survive; it is the
      large-sample reading of the question, nothing more.
Also reported: how many of the 15 reviewed losers each variant would have removed, next to how many WINNERS it removes - the number my eye could not see.
My guess, written now: on 02-27..04-22 the one-way-night days look terrible (that is where I found them); on 01-06..02-26 they are average or better; K3 fails for all three.
If so the honest lesson of this review is about the method: fifteen losers share whatever is common, and I need the winners next to them before I see anything.

## What I learned
- About the losers: since late February, mornings after a night that ran against my side have been poor (19 trades, 37% winners, -0.09R) and mornings after a night with
  me excellent (20 trades, 85% winners). In January-February there was almost no difference. I cannot tell whether the market changed or whether I am describing the
  same 15 trades I started from; the test was built to refuse exactly this evidence, and it did.
- About the method - the real lesson, and the coach's "slow down" is why I saw it: a losers-only review finds whatever is COMMON, not what is DIFFERENT. "12 of 15 losers
  had the night against them" sounded like a discovery; the base rate was 37 of 77 mornings, and the same days hold 19 winners. From now on a chart review of losers
  is always done with the same number of winners from the same weeks next to it, and the first thing written is the base rate of every feature on all days.
- What stays on the table without any action: the all-history gap for "night against me" is +0.55R against a noise band of +-0.46R, with 37 and 40 trades - the
  biggest-sample split I have, and just outside noise. It failed the line that matters (the part I did not choose by its losers), and skipping those days would have
